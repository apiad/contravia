Sky, night sky, weather and shadows

apiad/havana-driver (private)·takes day and night out of layer 6 of the havana-driver design and adds weather and shadows

The sun, the moon, the planets and the stars move over Havana where they really are at that minute of that day of the year, and buildings and cars cast their shadows. The weather follows a model built once from thirty years of hourly Havana data: dry months are mostly clear, and from May to October the clouds build up and it rains in the afternoon. The game needs no network to run it.

01What it delivers

You drive at 17:30 in September and the light is low and orange, the sky has filled with cloud, and a storm crosses the city for an hour. In January at the same hour the sky is clear and the sun sets at 18:10. At night the moon lights the streets according to its phase, and above the rooftops you see the real night sky: Orion rising in the east on a November evening, Polaris low in the north at 23°, Venus or Jupiter where they are that week, fewer stars when the moon is full or the sky is hazy. Shadows of buildings fall across the street in the morning and shrink at noon; in June at noon the sun is almost overhead and they nearly disappear. For manifoldx, the layer exercises a sun that moves every frame, a shadow map that follows a moving player over a city, a sky environment that changes over time, a dome of a few thousand star sprites turned by the sidereal clock, and rain drawn around the camera.

Numbers from a spike on the server: NOAA's solar position for Havana, and the ERA5 reanalysis for 1991 to 2020 (262 992 hourly records, none missing) from the Open-Meteo archive.

03How it fits

Two new modules beside clock.py: sky.py (pure functions of time and place) and weather.py (the chain and its table). A third, atmosphere.py, turns their output into engine calls each frame, so the first two are tested without a renderer. havana climate is the only part that touches the network.

04Try the sky

try it Looking south from a street in Havana, east on the left and west on the right. The sun's and moon's places use the formulas of section 05 in a short form, and the stars are the 1 618 BSC5 stars brighter than magnitude 5, turned by sidereal time (planets are left out of the sketch); the weather of the month is drawn from the ERA5 table of section 07 (cloud and chance of rain by hour are real; the drawing is a sketch, not the renderer).

05Sun and moon

  • sky.sun(t) returns the sun's height and bearing for Havana (23.136° N, 82.359° W) from NOAA's solar position equations: declination, equation of time and hour angle from the Julian day. The spike gives the June sunset at 20:19 and the December one at 17:50.
  • sky.moon(t) returns the moon's height, bearing and lit fraction from Meeus's low-precision series (chapter 47, the main terms only), good to about half a degree.
  • Time zone is America/Havana from the standard library's zoneinfo, so summer time changes the clock but not the sky.
  • The direction in the game's frame: x is east, z is south and y is up (havana/geo.py), so north is −z and the sun's bearing turns into the light direction with one rotation; a test checks that the morning sun comes from +x.

06Night sky

  • Stars: havana climate also reads BSC5 (Harvard's binary edition, 291 KB) and keeps stars brighter than magnitude 6.5, 8 369 of them, as right ascension, declination, magnitude and colour index in data/stars.npy, about 130 KB. Positions are moved from J2000 to the game's year by precession, about a third of a degree, so the pole star sits where it does today.
  • Turning the sky: local sidereal time from the clock and Havana's longitude gives one rotation from the equatorial frame to the game's frame. A spike for 23:00 on 15 November puts Polaris at 23.9°, Orion's belt at 35° in the east, Sirius rising at 12° and Vega just set, as an almanac shows.
  • Planets: Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter and Saturn from JPL's approximate orbital elements (Standish), with their brightness from distance and phase. They are sprites like the stars, brighter and unblinking.
  • Brightness: a star's sprite size and alpha come from its magnitude, and its colour from its colour index (blue-white Rigel, orange Betelgeuse). Atmospheric extinction dims stars near the horizon.
  • What you can see: a limiting magnitude decides which stars show. It starts from a city value of about 4.5 (some 500 stars above the horizon), rises with a clear moonless sky, falls with moonlight, twilight, haze and cloud, and is a setting, so a blackout night can show the full sky. Stars fade in through twilight from the brightest down, as the sun goes from −4° to −15°.
  • Dome: the sprites sit at a radius inside the camera's far plane, centred on the camera, drawn after the sky and before the city. The sprite path in manifoldx does not take fog, which the stars need; the plan checks this first and adds a flag if it does.
  • Moon: drawn as a disc with its real phase and the lit side toward the sun.
  • Milky Way: from NASA's Deep Star Maps 2020 (Scientific Visualization Studio, item 4851), the starless Milky Way image in the celestial frame, 1024×512 and 71 KB. havana climate samples it into about 25 000 faint sprites weighted by brightness, with their colour, in data/milkyway.npy (about 400 KB). They sit on the same dome as the stars and turn with them, so no texture and no rebuilt environment are needed. A test checks that the brightest sampled region is the galactic centre (right ascension 266°, declination −29°). The Milky Way shows only when the limiting magnitude passes about 5.5: on moonless, clear nights with the city glow low, such as a blackout.

07Climate data

havana climate downloads ERA5's hourly cloud_cover and precipitation for 1991–2020 at the grid point nearest Havana (the archive returns 23.0° N, 82.5° W, about 20 km south-west of the old city; 9.6 MB of JSON). ERA5 gets the shape right and the amounts wrong. Its year adds up to about 800 mm, while the Casablanca gauge's full months average 270 mm in June against ERA5's 134, with fewer rain days (12.5 against 17.5). That is ERA5's known bias on a coarse grid: too many hours of drizzle, too little in each storm. The game sees frequency and intensity, not millimetres, so the build corrects it in one place: per month, the threshold that counts an hour as "rain" rises until the rain days match the gauge, and the storm state takes the top tenth of rainy hours. The gauge numbers come from NOAA GHCN-Daily for CUM00078325 (1973–2016, 43 months with 26 or more days of data); official INSMET normals would replace them if we find them (open question).

08Weather model

  • Five states: clear (cloud under 20 %), partly cloudy (20–60 %), overcast (over 60 %), rain, storm. Each state stores its mean cloud cover and rain intensity for the month.
  • The table holds one 5×5 transition matrix per month and hour, 12×24×25 numbers, learnt by counting hour-to-hour transitions in ERA5. Months with too few counts in a cell borrow from the neighbouring hours.
  • The weather of a day is generated once when the day starts: the chain runs from three days earlier, starting from the month's average state, with random numbers seeded by the date. Within an hour the cloud and rain values blend from one state to the next over ten minutes, so a storm comes in rather than switching on.
  • weather.at(t) returns cloud cover (0–1), rain intensity (0–1), a wet-road value that rises with rain and dries over about an hour of sun, and the state's name for the HUD and the agent channel.

09Drawing

  • Sun light: direction from sky.sun; colour from white at noon to orange under 10° and red under 3°; intensity falls with height and with cloud by the Kasten–Czeplak factor 1 − 0.75·c3.4.
  • Moon light: when the sun is under the horizon, the directional light becomes the moon: pale blue, intensity scaled by the lit fraction, at most a few percent of the sun.
  • Sky and fill light: EnvironmentMap.from_sky with zenith, horizon and ground colours chosen from the sun's height and the cloud cover; it also draws the skybox. Rebuilt only when the colours move by more than a small step (about once a game minute), because the environment's precompute is not free.
  • Fog: its colour follows the horizon colour; in rain the far end comes from 650 m down to about 250 m, in a storm to about 150 m. The streaming radius does not change.
  • Rain: a few thousand thin quads instanced in a box around the camera, falling and wrapping, with their count following rain intensity. Wet roads: the road material turns darker and less rough with the wet value.
  • Clouds are not geometry in this layer: the sky goes grey, the sun disc fades, and the light drops.

10Shadows

manifoldx already has directional shadow mapping (engine.enable_shadows), which by default fits the whole scene. Here it runs with auto_fit=False:

  • An orthographic box of about 300 m around the player, at 2048 texels (about 15 cm each), with near and far set from the sun's direction and the city's tallest building.
  • The box's centre is snapped to whole texels in the light's frame, so static shadows stay still while the player moves.
  • Casters: buildings, trees, the player's car and traffic cars. Roads and ground only receive.
  • Shadows fade out as the sun goes under 5°, and with cloud: under an overcast sky the sun's share of light is small and the shadow map is not drawn at all, which also saves its cost.
  • No moon shadows.

11Settings and events

As with traffic, every number is a setting that can change while the game runs:

  • havana play --time "Sat 17:30" --date 2026-09-12 pins the clock; without them the game uses the machine's clock.
  • --climate my.json overrides parts of the climate: a rain factor and a cloud factor per month, the seed, or a fixed weather state.
  • The event sky_config changes the same keys during play; weather forces a state for a while ({"state": "storm", "minutes": 30}), for the menu and for agents testing.
  • havana skymap out.png --month 9 draws a year or a month of the model as a strip (hour across, day down, colour by state), to compare two climates the way havana flowmap compares two traffic settings.

12Measuring

  • havana bench gains columns for the sky update, the shadow pass and the rain, at 12:00 clear and 17:00 storm in September.
  • Budgets on a laptop GPU: sky and weather update under 0.5 ms per frame on the CPU; shadows and rain together within the frame budget the design sets. On the server, which draws in software, the bench reports their share of the frame instead of a hard number.

13Testing

  • Sun: sunrise, sunset and noon height on four dates against NOAA's published values for Havana, within two minutes and 0.2°.
  • Moon: phase on known full and new moons of 2026, and height against published rise and set times, within ten minutes.
  • Stars and planets: Polaris's height equals Havana's latitude within one degree at any time; the rise and set times of Sirius and of Venus on given dates match an almanac within five minutes.
  • Weather: simulate thirty years from the table; each month's rain days, mean cloud and likeliest rain hour must be within 10 % (or one hour) of what the table was built from.
  • Determinism: the same date and seed give the same weather; a different seed gives a different one.
  • Frame: the morning sun's light comes from the map's east; a shadow box snapped twice at positions a texel apart lands on the same grid.
  • Screenshots per milestone: Paseo at 08:00, 13:00 and 18:30 in November and June; a September storm; a full-moon night; a moonless November night with Orion over the Malecón.

14Done when

  • havana play without options shows the sky and weather of the machine's date and hour, with shadows, and needs no network.
  • The tests of section 13 pass, and the thirty-year simulation matches the table.
  • The bench shows the new costs at both test hours.
  • The milestone screenshots have been sent and approved.
  • The proof: havana timelapse renders 24 hours of one fixed date from a street in front of the Capitolio, looking at its dome, at one game minute per frame (1 440 frames, 48 s at 30 fps). The date is chosen so the video holds a sunrise, a sunset, an afternoon storm and the moon; the star limit is raised so the Milky Way shows at night. The command pins the clock and freezes the player; traffic runs as usual.

15Non-goals

  • Street lamps, the sea, the Malecón wall and trees by zone: they stay in layer 6.
  • Hurricanes and cold fronts as their own events; the chain gives long rainy spells but no wind or surge.
  • Flooded streets, puddles, wind on trees, volumetric clouds, lightning.
  • Stars twinkling, meteors, satellites, and planets' moons.
  • Weather effects on traffic (slower cars in rain); a later layer can read weather.at(t).
  • Live weather from the internet.

16Open questions

  • question INSMET's official monthly normals for Casablanca would replace the sparse gauge record in the ERA5 correction. Until someone finds them, the gauge record is used.
  • default Rain does not slow traffic in this layer; a later layer can read weather.at(t).
  • default Night is moonlight plus city glow; street lamps stay in layer 6.
